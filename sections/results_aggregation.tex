\subsection{Representation and time windows (H1)}
\label{sec:r-aggregation}
\paragraph{Contact and messaging networks.}
Every structural metric changes substantially with the window width in both networks
(\cref{tab:a-windows}, \cref{fig:agg-window}a,b). Density, clustering and LCC fraction
increase with the width, with sensitivities between 1.6 and 3.9; the number of weak
components in CM varies least (0.364).

\paragraph{Citation network.}
In 2020 the annual and cumulative graphs on the same 19,150 published papers contain 1,450
and 73,269 citations, with mean total degree 0.151 and 7.652 and LCC fraction 0.052 and
0.641, respectively. Cumulative clustering is 0.124, whereas annual clustering is
identically zero: because citations point to strictly older papers, every annual snapshot
is bipartite. This is a property of the representation and not evidence of temporal
correlation. Over trailing windows of 1--10 years, the sensitivities are 1.798 for density,
2.361 for clustering and 1.114 for the LCC fraction (\cref{fig:agg-window}c). Longer
windows connect a larger fraction of papers and admit triangles spanning several years,
which a single final aggregate cannot reveal.

\paragraph{Verdict on H1.}
H1 is supported in all three networks as a representation effect. The numerical
sensitivity depends on the window grid, the window alignment, the inclusion of inactive
nodes and the observation span, so the values in \cref{tab:a-windows} do not rank the
network types. The dependence on the observation interval is illustrated by the PS
clustering sensitivity, which decreases from 1.628 over the full period to 0.921 and 0.959
in the two halves.
